Unit 1: Business Activity

Entrepreneurship & Risk

  • Purpose of business activity: Spotting an opportunity, developing ideas, satisfying customer needs.
  • Characteristics of Entrepreneurs: Creativity, risk-taking, determination, confidence.
  • Risk and Reward: Higher risk can lead to higher profit/financial reward, but risk of total failure.

Business Ownership Types

  • Sole Trader: Owned by one person. Easy to set up, unlimited liability.
  • Partnership: 2–20 owners. Shared responsibility, shared capital, unlimited liability.
  • Private Limited Company (Ltd): Shares sold privately. Limited liability.
  • Public Limited Company (PLC): Shares traded publicly on stock exchange. High capital, risk of takeover.

Unit 2: Marketing

Market Research Methods

  • Primary Research (Field): First-hand data (questionnaires, focus groups, interviews, observation). Tailored specifically to business needs.
  • Secondary Research (Desk): Pre-existing data (government census, internet, newspapers, internal sales data). Cheaper and faster, but may be outdated.
  • Quantitative vs Qualitative: Quantitative = numerical data (how many?). Qualitative = opinions and reasons (why?).

The Marketing Mix (4 Ps) & Pricing Strategies

  • Product, Price, Place, Promotion.
  • Pricing Strategies: Penetration (low initial price for market share), Skimming (high price for new tech/luxury), Cost-plus (markup on production cost), Competitive pricing, Promotional pricing.
  • Product Life Cycle: Introduction, Growth, Maturity, Decline. Extensions strategies keep products alive.

Unit 3: People (Human Resources)

Organisational Structures & Communication

  • Tall structures: Many layers, long chain of command, narrow span of control.
  • Flat structures: Few layers, wide span of control, rapid communication, delegation.
  • Span of control: Number of subordinates directly reporting to a manager.

Recruitment, Training & Motivation

  • Financial motivation: Piece rate, commission, bonus, profit-sharing.
  • Non-financial motivation: Fringe benefits, job rotation, job enrichment, training.
  • Training types: Induction (new staff), On-the-job, Off-the-job.

Unit 4: Operations

Production Processes & Quality

  • Job production: Unique, single items made to customer specifications (e.g., wedding cake, tailored suit).
  • Batch production: Groups of identical items made together in stages.
  • Flow/Mass production: Continuous assembly line production of standardized goods.
  • Quality Control: Checking products at the end of the production process.
  • Quality Assurance: Building quality into every stage of production (Total Quality Management).

E-commerce, Logistics & Consumer Law

  • E-commerce: Selling online 24/7 globally, though faces high delivery and cyber security costs.
  • Consumer Rights Act: Goods must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, and as described.

Unit 5: Finance

Sources of Finance

  • Internal: Retained profit, sale of assets, owner's capital. No interest payments.
  • External: Bank loans, overdrafts, share capital, venture capital, trade credit, crowdfunding, leasing.
  • Short-term (overdraft, trade credit) for day-to-day needs; long-term (loan, shares) for big investments.

Cash Flow & Break-even

  • Cash vs profit: Cash is money in the bank now; profit is revenue minus costs. A profitable business can still run out of cash.
  • Cash flow forecast: Predicts inflows/outflows. Helps spot shortages and get loans.
  • Break-even: Output where revenue = total costs. Margin of safety = actual output − break-even output.

Profit & Investment

  • Gross profit = revenue − cost of sales. Net profit = gross profit − expenses.
  • ARR compares projects: higher % = better return on the money invested.

Unit 6: Influences on Business

Ethical & Environmental

  • Ethics: Fair pay, honest advertising, ethical sourcing. Can build reputation but raises costs.
  • Environment: Pollution, waste, carbon footprint, sustainability. Pressure groups can damage a brand.

Economic Climate

  • Interest rates: Rise = loans cost more, consumers spend less.
  • Inflation: Rising prices raise costs. Exchange rates: Strong £ makes exports dearer, imports cheaper.
  • Unemployment / recession: Lower consumer spending and demand.

Globalisation & Legislation

  • Globalisation: Bigger markets and cheaper production, but more competition and cultural/ethical issues.
  • Law: Consumer Rights Act, Equality Act, Health & Safety at Work Act, National Minimum Wage, competition law.

Unit 7: Interdependence of Functions

How Functions Link

  • Marketing ↔ Operations: A big promotion boosts demand, so production and stock must cope.
  • Finance ↔ HR: Hiring and training cost money; budgets limit recruitment.
  • Operations ↔ Finance: Higher quality or new machines raise costs but can cut waste.

Evaluation Skills

  • Trade-offs: Gaining one benefit means giving up another (quality vs cost, speed vs accuracy).
  • Always link to the case study, weigh pros and cons, then justify a decision and consider short vs long term.

The 3-mark “Analyse” question

Takes about 3 minutes. You develop one reason, impact or factor. Three marks = three links in a chain.

① KNOWLEDGE
State the point
② APPLY
Link to the business
③ ANALYSE
So what? Consequence
Context: Bella's Bakes is a sole trader in Leeds. It sells bread, cakes and wedding cakes and employs 4 staff. Bella wants to grow the business.

Analyse one benefit to Bella's Bakes of using social media to promote its cakes. 3 marks

Social media lets a business reach a large audience at a low cost. Bella could post photos of her wedding cakes on Instagram, which engaged couples in Leeds are likely to see. This would raise awareness of the bakery, leading to more orders and therefore higher revenue.

Chain words

This means…
which leads to…
so…
this would…
therefore…
as a result…

Time & length

2–3 sentences. Don't repeat the question or list extra points: one well-developed point beats three vague ones.

Weak vs strong

1 mark: “Social media is good because loads of people use it and it's cheap.”
True but generic. No business, no consequence.
3 marks: “It's cheap, so Bella can promote her cakes without a big advertising budget. Posting wedding cakes on Instagram reaches local couples, which could lead to more orders and higher revenue.”

3-mark “Recommend / Evaluate”

Bella needs £8,000 for new ovens. Recommend whether she should use a bank loan or retained profit.

1Decision: “I recommend Bella uses a bank loan.”
2Justify with the context: “A small bakery would take months to save £8,000 from profit, but a loan gives the whole amount now…”
3Consequence: “…so the new ovens can increase production straight away and raise sales.”

The 7-mark “Discuss / Evaluate”

Takes about 7–8 minutes. Marks reward application (use the case study), analysis (logical chains) and evaluation (a supported judgement for this business).

1Point for (K → A → AN): one developed argument in favour.
2Point against (K → A → AN): one developed counter-argument. “However…”
3Judgement: “Overall, I think… because…” Make a clear decision.
4Justify it: say why this matters most for this business, or when your decision would change.
Context: Bella is a sole trader. Her bakery is busy and she has just bought £12,000 of ovens on credit.

Bella is thinking about becoming a private limited company. Discuss whether she should. 7 marks

A private limited company gives its owners limited liability. Because Bella has bought £12,000 of ovens on credit, if the bakery failed she could lose her own savings or home as a sole trader. Limited liability would protect her personal assets, reducing her risk and giving her the confidence to expand.
However, limited companies must follow more legal rules and publish their accounts. Bella currently runs the bakery alone and would need an accountant to complete the paperwork. This would add to her costs and take time away from baking, so it could reduce her profit.
Overall, I think Bella should become a private limited company. Her large ovens debt makes the protection of limited liability more important than the extra paperwork, which an accountant could handle. This is especially true while she is expanding, though if she planned to stay small and avoid borrowing, remaining a sole trader might be better.

Judgement starters

  • Overall, I think… because…
  • The most important factor is… because…
  • This depends on… but in this case…
  • In the short term… but in the long term…

Don't

  • Sit on the fence (“both are good”)
  • Repeat your points in the conclusion
  • Write generic points that fit any business

Weak vs strong judgement

“In conclusion, both have advantages and disadvantages so it depends.”
No decision, no reason.
“Overall Bella should become a Ltd because her ovens debt makes limited liability the most important factor for her, outweighing the extra paperwork.”

The 9-mark “Evaluate”

Takes about 9–10 minutes. The marks are weighted towards evaluation, so don't spend all your time describing.

1 K
1 U
2 APPLY
2 ANALYSE
3 EVALUATE

Knowledge · Understanding · Application · Analysis · Evaluation. In Paper 2 the 9-marker is synoptic, so it can bring in ideas from across the course (e.g. finance, operations and marketing together).

Context: Bella's Bakes is popular locally for fresh wedding cakes. She wants to grow and is choosing between opening a second shop in Headingley and selling cakes online.

Evaluate which option Bella should choose. 9 marks

Opening a second shop gives a business access to more customers in a new area. Because Bella's wedding cakes are already well known across Leeds, a Headingley shop could attract new customers, increasing sales and revenue. However, she would need to pay rent, buy equipment and hire staff and managing two sites with a team of four could stretch her, lowering quality.
Selling online lets a business reach customers 24/7 across the UK without the cost of premises. Bella could sell boxed cupcakes nationwide, which keeps fixed costs low and could grow profit quickly. However, delivering fragile cakes is costly and risky, and damaged cakes could harm her reputation.
Overall, I recommend the second shop. The most important factor is protecting Bella's reputation for fresh, hand-finished cakes, which delivery could damage. Although a second shop costs more up front, it fits her aim of steady growth with limited risk. In the long run, once the shop is profitable, she could add online sales to spread her risk.
